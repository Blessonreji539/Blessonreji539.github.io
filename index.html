01 / INTRODUCTION

About me.

I love engineering because it turns curiosity into something real.

For me, engineering is more than a job title. I enjoy asking why something works, finding what is preventing it from working better, and bringing different pieces together into a dependable solution. Whether I am testing a circuit, writing control logic, reviewing a mechanical design, or troubleshooting a prototype, the most rewarding moment is when an idea becomes a working system.

At Temple University, I explored embedded systems, industrial automation, electrical circuits, manufacturing, and engineering design. Leading a three-person capstone team on SeaSPRITE showed me how much I enjoy the intersection of technical problem-solving, collaboration, and system integration.

My internship in construction materials testing added another dimension: engineering decisions must stand up to real-world conditions, safety requirements, deadlines, and communication with people on site. Those experiences shaped my interest in systems engineering and controls.

What makes my approach different is that I enjoy seeing the whole picture. I want to understand how individual components interact, how an issue affects the larger system, and how to make that system more reliable and useful. I am committed to continuing to learn, building hands-on skills, and contributing to meaningful engineering work.

FEATURED SENIOR CAPSTONE · 2025

SeaSPRITE — Digital Modernization of a Naval Surface Vessel

Build an affordable, modular wireless controller capable of at least 50 meters of reliable operation.

Explore case study ↗

Our ENGT 4196 senior design team collaborated with NSWCPD to develop a modular wireless control architecture for educational aquatic robotics. I led the three-person team and contributed to hardware integration, troubleshooting, testing, documentation and design trade-offs. The prototype combined legacy joystick inputs, ESP32 firmware, wireless communication, motor actuation and power management. We prioritized functional validation, cost awareness, electrical safety and repeatable student assembly. The project demonstrated a working surface-vehicle prototype and established a foundation for future PCB and water-based validation.

Technical skills: Systems integration, Electrical power distribution, Embedded control, Motor and steering control

Controls Engineering · 2025

PLC & Industrial Automation

Program and verify PLC logic, analog signal monitoring, and motor speed control.

Explore case study ↗

I designed, wired, programmed, and troubleshot Allen-Bradley Micro820 PLC training systems at Temple University. Using Connected Components Workbench, I developed ladder logic for digital I/O, relay logic, timers, counters, and sequential operations. I also used analog signals, comparisons, MOV instructions, and voltage thresholds to control indicator outputs. Another exercise integrated a variable frequency drive with pushbutton start-stop controls and potentiometer-based speed regulation. This work built practical skills in industrial control wiring, PLC testing, motor control, and troubleshooting.

Technical skills: PLC programming, Ladder logic, Digital and analog I/O, 24 VDC wiring

Design a functional 3D-printed track informed by kinematics.

Electrical Engineering · 2022

Automatic Night Light Circuit

Design and analyze a photoresistor-controlled night light.

Explore case study ↗

I developed an automatic night-light circuit concept using a photoresistor and LED. The design explored how changes in ambient light could produce an electrical response. I applied Ohm's Law, Kirchhoff's Laws, voltage dividers, Thevenin's theorem, and Wheatstone bridge analysis. I used NI Multisim to simulate resistance changes and evaluate circuit behavior. The project strengthened my DC circuit analysis and simulation skills.

Technical skills: DC circuit analysis, Wheatstone bridge analysis, Voltage dividers, Thevenin equivalent circuits

Sustainability · Research · 2020

Water Infrastructure Design — Peru

Compare feasible conceptual water-infrastructure interventions.

Explore case study ↗

I collaborated with a three-member team on a conceptual water-infrastructure design challenge focused on Lobitos and Piedritas, Peru. We evaluated water treatment, local storage and pumping, and rainwater collection alternatives. Using a weighted multi-criteria assessment in Microsoft Excel, we compared social, economic, environmental, and implementation factors. Rainwater collection stations received the highest score in our conceptual comparison. The project taught me to evaluate engineering alternatives while considering community needs and practical constraints.

Technical skills: Multi-criteria decision analysis, Sustainable infrastructure, Feasibility analysis, Stakeholder considerations

Biomedical Design · Research · 2020

Portable Liver Transport System

Investigate design constraints and risk-reduction concepts for donor-liver transport.

Explore case study ↗

I collaborated on an engineering design research project examining safer transportation and preservation of donor livers. The team researched conventional cold storage and normothermic machine perfusion concepts. We identified stakeholder needs and constraints involving transport duration, power supply, equipment size, and reliability. We assessed potential hazards such as power overload and pump failure and considered redundant systems. The project was a conceptual research and design exercise, not a clinically validated medical device.
